## Security model (reworked 2026-08-06 — read before touching auth or RLS)

- **RLS is the ONLY real barrier. `role?.isAdmin` in the UI is cosmetic.** PostgreSQL ORs permissive policies together, so one broad policy defeats every narrow one on the same table. `finance_entries` and `employee_hours` each had an admin policy AND a blanket "company read" policy — meaning **any signed-in crew member could read all company money and everyone's pay rates straight from the REST API**. Measured before the fix: 4 viewer accounts could see 110 finance rows ($56,617.26 of payments) and 51 hours rows exposing 6 pay rates. Migration `2026-08-06_rls_money_lockdown.sql` drops both. Verified by impersonating a viewer (`set local role authenticated` + jwt claims): finance 110→**0**, hours 51→**5** (own only), jobs and customers unchanged. **`jobs company read` and `customers company read` were kept on purpose** — crew need those for field work.
- **`employees` is admin-assigned only, structurally.** RLS is enabled with **zero** insert/update/delete policies, so no client key can ever write to it — only the service role (dashboard). Signing up therefore cannot grant staff access whatever the client sends.
- **Everyone who self-signs-up is a CUSTOMER** (`2026-08-06_customer_accounts.sql`). A `SECURITY DEFINER` trigger on `auth.users` files any new user without a matching `employees` row into `customer_accounts`. Verified both ways in a rolled-back transaction, including an UPPERCASE staff email to confirm the match is case-insensitive. Customers currently have access to nothing: their only policy is reading their own `customer_accounts` row.
- **Routing vs security:** `lib/account.ts::getAccountInfo` returns `employee | customer | none | unknown` and is used for ROUTING ONLY. **`unknown` means a session exists but classification failed (usually no signal), and the tabs gate FAILS OPEN on it** — the crew work in dead zones, and bouncing someone to the login screen mid-job while holding a valid session would be a worse bug than a customer briefly seeing empty screens. RLS decides what actually loads either way. `(tabs)/_layout.tsx` now gates on it — before 2026-08-06 the tab routes had **no gate at all**, so anyone signed in (or typing /pipeline on web) reached the crew shell.
- **Account deletion** (App Store 5.1.1(v)): edge function `delete-account` (verify_jwt ON, service role, takes the user id from the verified JWT and requires `{"confirm":"DELETE"}`). Offered on the customer screen and in More. It deletes the LOGIN, not the `employees` roster row — that's Devon's employment record and the jobs/hours/finance history reference it.
- **Password policy (Supabase Pro, enabled 2026-08-06):** min 10, requires upper + lower + digit, **leaked-password (HIBP) check ON**, email OTP expiry cut 60 min → 10 min. HIBP needs Pro; the other three work on Free.
- **Session tokens are NOT httpOnly** — `localStorage` on web, `AsyncStorage` on native. That's the Supabase default and fine today, but it means XSS equals account takeover: be careful adding third-party scripts to the web app, especially once a public marketing portal shares the origin.
- **Two-factor for STAFF (2026-08-06):** `lib/mfa.ts` + `app/security.tsx` (More → Security & 2FA), with the login challenge in `app/index.tsx`. Password + TOTP. **No "remember me" toggle was needed** — verifying raises the SESSION to aal2 and it stays there until sign-out, so it's once per device already. QR renders on web; on the phone the setup key is shown selectable (`expo-clipboard` is NOT installed and would force a native build). An abandoned enrolment is cleared on the next attempt, so a half-finished setup can never lock anyone out.

## Customer portal (2026-08-06)

- **Invite → link → read.** Devon taps **Invite** on a customer in More → Customers. That calls the `invite-customer` edge function (admin-only, service role), which uses Supabase's admin invite and stamps `customer_id` into the new user's metadata. The `handle_new_auth_user` trigger reads it, so the account arrives **already linked** to the CRM record. Linking at invite time is deliberate: a customer never types who they are, so nobody can claim someone else's projects by guessing an email. The button reads "In portal" once a login exists.
- **What a customer may see is decided in SQL, not the UI:** `my_projects()`, `my_documents()`, `my_balance()` — all SECURITY DEFINER, granted to `authenticated` only, revoked from `anon`. Customers have **no direct table access** to `jobs` or `finance_entries` at all; the functions choose the rows AND the columns, so a customer can't widen the query.
- **Expenses are excluded server-side** (`type in ('invoice','estimate','payment')`). That is our cost base — a homeowner must never see what we paid for their materials. Do not "fix" this by filtering in the client.
- Verified by standing up a throwaway linked login in a rolled-back transaction: it saw **10 of 27** jobs, **21 of 110** finance rows and **0 of 57** expenses.
- 12 of 16 customers have an email today; the other 4 need one before they can be invited (the function says so rather than failing silently).
- Customers are **web-first** by intent, but nothing blocks iOS — the same screens work once the app ships.

- **Offline / weak-signal banner (2026-08-06):** `lib/connection.ts` + `components/ConnectionBanner.tsx`, mounted in the ROOT layout so it floats over every screen. Three states — online / slow (>3.5 s) / offline — decided by polling `/auth/v1/health` (20 s when healthy, 6 s when not, plus an immediate re-check when the app foregrounds and on the web `online`/`offline` events). **No netinfo dependency on purpose**: that's native, and it would have cost a full App Store build instead of an OTA.
